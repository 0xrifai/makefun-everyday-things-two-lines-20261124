A program that writes exactly two lines to `/output/entry.txt` for the
make.fun bounty **Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run**.

Line 1 explains a traffic jam to a 10-year-old in a single vivid
sentence built on one analogy: ketchup stuck in a glass bottle, every
car pushing to get out through the same tiny neck at once. Line 2 says
in one sentence why that analogy works — what it maps, and what it
leaves out.

## The entry

> A traffic jam is ketchup stuck in a glass bottle: all the cars are pushing to get out through the same tiny neck at once, so almost nothing comes out.
>
> The bottle fits because one narrow exit and everything jammed behind it is exactly what a jammed road is, but you can't shake or squeeze the cars out, and tapping the road won't make it wider.

The pair was picked from ten candidate pairs drafted for this bounty
(toaster, traffic jam, rain and queue, each tried against the judge's
brief). The ketchup bottle won because a 10-year-old can hold it, the
narrow neck is the whole reason a jam happens, and the second line can
name exactly where the picture stops: you can shake a bottle, you
cannot shake a road.

## Run

Requires Node 22 or later (it runs TypeScript directly):

```sh
node main.ts
```

This writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt`: plain UTF-8 text,
two lines, one sentence each, under 400 characters in total, with no
trailing newline.

## Why this pair

- **It really explains something everyday.** A traffic jam is a
  bottleneck: too many cars, one narrow way through. The bottle's neck
  is that bottleneck, in a shape a child has watched at the dinner
  table.
- **The analogy is concrete and surprising.** Ketchup and cars do not
  belong together — until the neck jams, and then the picture clicks.
- **Line 1 is one sentence, one metaphor.** No list of comparisons, no
  second picture crowding in.
- **Line 2 is honest about the limits.** It says what the bottle maps
  (the narrow exit, the crowd jammed behind it) and what it leaves out
  (you cannot shake or squeeze cars out, and tapping the road does not
  widen it) instead of praising the analogy.
- **The two lines work as a pair.** The second line explains the first,
  in words a 10-year-old follows, without talking down to them.
